# Your Website, Explained

A plain-English guide to what a static website is, how it compares to what you have now (WordPress + Elementor), and what managing one actually looks like day-to-day.

---

## TL;DR

1. **Static site** = faster, safer, cheaper, and lasts longer without breaking. Editing requires a small request to your developer (or a simple editor tool we set up for you).
2. **WordPress + Elementor** = you can edit things yourself in a visual builder, but you pay for it in speed, security risk, ongoing maintenance, and monthly costs.
3. For a site like yours that doesn't change daily, **static is the better call.**

---

## What Is a Static Website?

A static website is a set of pre-built pages (HTML, CSS, images) that sit on a server and get delivered instantly to anyone who visits. Nothing is assembled on the fly. Nothing talks to a database. It just loads.

Think of it like a printed brochure that lives on the internet. Fast, simple, hard to break.

---

## What Is WordPress + Elementor?

WordPress is software that builds pages **on demand** every time someone visits. It pulls your content from a database, runs plugins, and assembles the page in real time. Elementor is a visual drag-and-drop builder that sits on top of WordPress.

This is powerful, but it's also a lot of moving parts. Every moving part is something that can break, slow down, or get hacked.

## The Security Difference (The Big One)

This is the part most clients don't fully grasp until something goes wrong.

### WordPress is a hacker magnet

1. WordPress powers around **40% of the internet**. That makes it the #1 target.
2. Every plugin is a potential door. Most sites have 15-30 plugins.
3. If you don't update WordPress, the theme, and every plugin **constantly**, known vulnerabilities pile up.
4. Common attacks include malware injections, spam redirects, credit card skimmers, and full site takeovers.
5. A hacked WordPress site can blacklist your domain in Google, tank your SEO, and expose customer data.

### Static sites are almost un-hackable

1. There's no login page. No database. No admin panel. No plugins.
2. There's nothing running on the server except delivering files.
3. Attackers have essentially **no surface to attack**.
4. The hosting providers we use (Cloudflare, Netlify) also sit behind enterprise-grade protection for free.

**Plain version:** A WordPress site is a house with 30 doors and windows, and you have to keep them all locked every day. A static site is a solid concrete block.

## What Managing a Static Site Actually Looks Like

### Day to day
Nothing. You don't do anything. The site just works.

### When you want to change something

1. Send a note to your developer: "Update the phone number on the contact page" or "Swap the hero photo."
2. Developer makes the change (usually 10-30 minutes of work).
3. Change goes live in under a minute.

Or, if we set up a lightweight content editor for you, you log in and change specific fields yourself (headlines, photos, prices). We decide together which fields are editable.

### What you'll never have to do again

1. Click "Update" on 20 plugins every week.
2. Pay a developer to fix a site that broke because two plugins don't like each other.
3. Restore from a backup after a hack.
4. Renew Elementor Pro, WP Rocket, or any of the other plugin licenses.
5. Panic when your hosting company emails you about a security issue.

## What You Give Up

Being honest about tradeoffs.

1. **You can't drag and drop new sections yourself.** If you want a whole new layout, the developer builds it.
2. **No native blog with a button to publish.** We can still add a blog to a static site, but it works differently. If you plan to publish articles weekly, tell us up front so we pick the right setup.
3. **No huge plugin ecosystem.** Anything custom (booking forms, calculators, member areas) is built deliberately, not installed.

For a service business like yours, none of these are dealbreakers.

2. Domain: $15/year (same as now)
3. Maintenance: basically nothing
4. **Realistic total: $15-$250/year**

## Where Your Site Lives

A static site doesn't need a fancy host. We recommend **Cloudflare Pages** or **Netlify**. Both are free for sites your size, fast worldwide, and essentially impossible to take offline.

The actual site files live in a **Git repository** (think: a version-controlled folder in the cloud, usually on GitHub). Every change is tracked. Every version is recoverable. If something goes wrong, we roll back in one click.

You don't have to touch any of this. It's just worth knowing your site isn't trapped on one person's laptop.

---

## What Happens If You Fire Your Developer

With WordPress, you often get stuck because only the original developer knows which plugins do what.

With a static site, the code is **just HTML, CSS, and images** in a Git repo. Any developer on earth can pick it up and keep going. You own it completely. No lock-in.

## Bottom Line

If your site needs to change multiple times a day by non-technical staff, WordPress makes sense. If your site is a digital brochure that represents your business and needs to load fast, stay online, stay secure, and not eat your time or money, **static is the clear winner**.

For your business, static is the right call.

---

## Questions To Ask Yourself Before Deciding

1. How often do I actually update my site today? (Most clients say "rarely.")
2. Who updates it? Me, or someone I pay?
3. Has my site ever broken, gotten hacked, or had issues after a plugin update?
4. Am I paying for WordPress maintenance I don't fully understand?
5. Do I want to spend time managing a website, or running my business?

If the honest answers point toward "rarely, someone I pay, yes, probably, running my business" — static is your answer.
